## Overview

In this class I learned the math behind the serial and parallel robots used in factories: finding where a 6-axis arm's end effector is, solving for the joint angles that get it somewhere, and spotting where the robot loses the ability to move. In the labs, I put it into practice on real industrial robots.

## Concepts

- **Robot types and workspaces:** serial arms (articulated, SCARA, spherical, cylindrical, Cartesian) versus parallel robots, and the volume each one can actually reach
- **Forward kinematics:** Denavit–Hartenberg parameters and chained homogeneous transforms from the base to the tool; a spherical wrist is just a ZYZ Euler rotation
- **Inverse kinematics by decoupling:** solve for the wrist center's position with the first three joints (geometry and the law of cosines, giving elbow-up and elbow-down solutions), then for orientation with the wrist's Euler angles
- **Velocity kinematics:** the Jacobian maps joint velocities to tool velocities, $\dot{\mathbf{p}} = J(\mathbf{q})\,\dot{\mathbf{q}}$, and inverting it gives the joint speeds for a desired tool motion
- **Singularities:** when the Jacobian loses rank, the arm loses a direction of motion and nearby joint speeds blow up; at the edge of the workspace, with two axes lined up, or when the wrist's first and last axes align
- **Numerical IK and the analytical Jacobian:** iterative Jacobian-based solvers for arms with no closed-form answer, and relating angular velocity to Euler-angle rates
